The Method

Every project follows the same three steps. The scope changes. The process doesn't.

Step one: research the buyer

STEP 01

Before writing anything

Three questions have to be answered before I write a word:

  • Who is the buyer? Not a demographic. The specific person with the specific problem.
  • How crowded is their market? If they've seen twenty offers like this one, the page has to work harder to be believed.
  • What do they already know? Whether they know they have a problem, know solutions exist, or have never thought about it — each requires a different page.

If you don't know where the reader is, you can't write the page that picks them up and moves them forward. I do this research myself.

Step two: lock one message

STEP 02

Seven decisions, made once

Before drafting, I lock seven things. Every section of the page will extract from these. If something doesn't serve them, it gets cut.

1. The problem

The specific, urgent pain the buyer feels.

2. The reframe

The idea that changes how they see the solution.

3. The offer

What they get, and why it's worth more than the price.

4. The difference

What you have that competitors don't.

5. The promise

The outcome, specific and time-bound.

6. The mechanism

The named process that makes it work.

7. The proof

Evidence a skeptical buyer would accept.

If there's no proof yet, that's a business problem before it's a copy problem. Better to know that now than to write a page nobody believes.

Step three: write from that message

STEP 03

One structure, every time

Every letter and every page follows the same order: headline, opening, the problem, the mechanism, proof, offer, objections, close.

Each section does one job. The headline stops the right reader. The opening shows I understand their situation. The mechanism explains how the outcome happens. The proof makes it credible. The close makes the decision clear.

Nothing gets added because it sounds good. Everything earns its place by moving the reader one step closer to acting.

The tools

I use AI the way a builder uses tools: for leverage, not shortcuts. Every word that ships is mine.

  • ResearchLive market research: competitor positioning, real buyer language from forums and communities.
  • VoiceExisting brand assets analysed for tone, so new copy doesn't sound like it came from a different company.
  • DraftingStructured drafting against the locked message.
  • AuditA separate pass checking every draft for weak logic, missed objections, and voice drift before delivery.

Why the message comes first

Most people who are unhappy with their conversion rate assume they need more traffic or a redesign. Usually they need the message fixed. The ad, the page, and the follow-up are telling three different stories, and the buyer feels the mismatch even if they can't name it.

Fix the message and everything downstream gets easier: the ads, the emails, the sales calls. That's why I start there.
